# Hypothesis Ledger

Opened 2026-10-24. Append-only below the decision record; never rewrite history.

---

## 0. Correction to the starting idea (made BEFORE choosing a hypothesis)

The prompt's starting idea was:

> "Probability evolution can be compatible with multiple velocity fields. In learned
> flow-matching models, this freedom might produce consequential differences in particle
> trajectories, even when training loss and generated endpoint distributions are similar."

**The first clause is true and already published** (Lim 2025, arXiv:2512.16768, Prop. 5.4;
and classically via Benamou–Brenier / Helmholtz in `L²(p_t)`). We may not claim it.

**The second clause contains a logical gap that must be fixed before it can be tested.**
The CFM objective is a least-squares regression onto the target `u = x_1 − x_0`:

    L(θ) = E_{t, x_0, x_1} ‖v_θ(t, x_t) − (x_1 − x_0)‖² ,   x_t = (1−t)x_0 + t x_1 .

Pointwise in `(t,x)` this is strictly convex in the value `v_θ(t,x)`, so its minimiser over
an unrestricted function class is the **unique** conditional expectation
`v*(t,x) = E[x_1 − x_0 | x_t = x]`, determined `dt ⊗ p_t`-a.e. (Lim 2025, Prop. 4.3, proves
exactly this for the empirical case.) **Therefore the CFM loss has no gauge freedom.** The
non-uniqueness of the continuity equation is *not* a non-uniqueness of the training
objective, and a model at the optimum cannot "choose" a different flux-equivalent field.

Two models with identical loss have (up to `p_t`-null sets) the *same* field. So the
starting idea cannot be true in the form "models pick different flux-equivalent fields."

**What survives, and is genuinely open.** Any trained model sits at `v_θ = v* + e` with
`e ≠ 0`. Decompose the residual orthogonally in `L²(p_t)`:

    e_t = ∇φ_t + r_t ,   ∇·(p_t r_t) = 0 ,   ⟨∇φ_t, r_t⟩_{L²(p_t)} = 0 .

Then (Huang et al., ICML 2025, Prop. 3.1, whose forcing term is exactly `−∇·(p_t e_t)`):

* `r_t` contributes **zero** to the evolution of the marginals — invisible to every
  distributional metric, to first order and, if `r` is flux-null for all `t`, exactly;
* `r_t` contributes **in full** to the ODE trajectories and hence to the transport map.

So the research question is not *"which field did the model pick?"* but:

> **Where in the residual error space does a trained network's error actually live, and
> how consequential is that location?**

This is the question the closest prior work explicitly declines to answer: Lim (2025)
states *"All experiments evaluate the closed-form empirical velocity directly; no neural
network is trained"* and lists neural-network approximation as open future work.

## 1. Candidate hypotheses

Notation used throughout. `p_1` = data, `p_0 = N(0,I)`, independent coupling, linear
interpolant. `v*` = population marginal velocity (closed form in our testbed, see
`theory.md`). `Φ*` = ground-truth flow map from integrating `v*` to tight tolerance.
`e = v_θ − v*`. `f` = time-integrated flux-null fraction
`f = ∫‖r_t‖²_{L²(p_t)} dt / ∫‖e_t‖²_{L²(p_t)} dt ∈ [0,1]`.
`M = E_{x_0}‖Φ_θ(x_0) − Φ*(x_0)‖₂` (map error, shared `x_0`).
`f_0` = flux-null fraction of a **matched null model** (see §3).

---

### H1 — Low-density under-fitting makes the residual disproportionately flux-null

**Claim (falsifiable).** For CFM networks trained to convergence on a 2-D multimodal
testbed, `f > f_0` by more than 0.05 in absolute fraction, and the excess `(f − f_0)` is
spatially concentrated in low-`p_t` regions between modes and temporally at `t` where
component posteriors compete.

**Mathematical reasoning.** Lim (2025) Prop. 5.3 shows the non-gradient part of `v*` is
generated by *spatial variation of the posterior weights* `w_i(t,x)`: `v*` is a gradient
field iff `Σ_i (v_i ∇w_iᵀ − ∇w_i v_iᵀ) = 0`. Posterior weights vary fastest where two or
more components have comparable responsibility — i.e. on the *inter-modal separatrices*,
which are exactly the **low-density** regions of `p_t`. The CFM loss weights squared error
by `p_t`. Hence the part of `v*` that carries the rotational/shear structure sits precisely
where the objective supplies the least gradient signal. A capacity- or optimisation-limited
network should therefore under-fit the flux-null structure of `v*` preferentially, leaving a
residual whose flux-null share exceeds chance.

**Predicted observation.** `f ≈ 0.6–0.8` against `f_0 ≈ 0.5`; a spatial map of
`‖r_t‖²/‖e_t‖²` lighting up the separatrices; `f` largest at intermediate-to-late `t`.

**Falsifying result.** `f` within ±0.05 of `f_0`, or `f < f_0`. Also falsified if `f > f_0`
but the excess is spatially uniform (then it is not the posterior-competition mechanism,
and some other explanation is needed).

**Honest prior.** The CFM loss penalises `‖∇φ‖²` and `‖r‖²` **equally** (orthogonality),
so there is *no* first-order optimisation asymmetry. H1 relies entirely on the second-order
claim that the two components live in different places and the loss weight differs there.
This is plausible but could easily fail. **My genuine prior is ~40% that H1 survives**; the
modal alternative outcome is `f ≈ f_0 ≈ 0.5`, which is a clean negative result on H1.

**Closest existing research.** Lim 2025 (flux-null space, non-gradient `v*` — theory only,
no networks); Li et al. 2026 kinetic-energy (low-density traversal costs energy — no
decomposition). **Why it might already be known:** it is a natural next step from Lim 2025
and shares an author with the ICML 2026 kinetic-energy paper. Mitigation: the targeted
full-text searches in `literature_matrix.md` §3 found *zero* papers decomposing a learned
FM velocity error into gradient/divergence-free parts.

**Minimum distinguishing experiment.** E1 (§3). Requires exact `v*` (closed form) and a
weighted-Helmholtz solver. **Cost:** ~1–2 CPU-hours.

**Confounders.** (a) Discretisation of the decomposition operator — handled by exact
discrete orthogonality plus a grid-refinement study. (b) Density floor in low-`p_t` regions
— handled by the `p_t`-weighted norm itself, which down-weights the tails, plus explicit
floor-sensitivity sweep. (c) `t→1` singularity of `v*` — handled by restricting to
`t ∈ [0, 1−δ]` and sweeping `δ`. (d) The null level `f_0` is a *modelling choice*; must be
estimated empirically with matched smoothness, not assumed to be ½.

### H2 — Distributional fidelity does not control transport-map fidelity (a measurable blindness factor)

**Claim.** `R = M / (W₂(Φ_θ#p_0, p_1) − W₂_floor) ≥ 3` for the median seed, and `R` does
**not** decrease as model capacity and training length increase. Equivalently: driving the
endpoint distributional error to the sampling-noise floor leaves a map error bounded below
by a quantity proportional to `‖r‖`.

**Mathematical reasoning.** First-order: `δp_1` is driven only by `∇·(p_t e_t)`, i.e. only
by `∇φ`. Map error is driven by all of `e` and is amplified by a Gronwall factor
`exp(∫‖∇v‖ dt)`. If `f` is bounded away from 0 — which holds *whether or not H1 holds*,
since `f_0 ≈ ½` — then a fixed fraction of velocity error is structurally undetectable by
endpoint metrics.

**Predicted observation.** `M` plateaus while `W₂` continues to fall across training
checkpoints; `R` large and roughly flat in capacity; between-seed map disagreement ≫
between-seed distributional disagreement.

**Falsifying result.** `R ≲ 1`, or `R` shrinking systematically with capacity/training —
either would mean distributional metrics do effectively bound map fidelity.

**Honest prior.** ~75% that H2 survives. It is much weaker than H1 and follows almost from
`f_0 ≈ ½` plus Gronwall. Its value is **quantitative** (how large is the blindness factor,
and is it stable?), not conceptual.

**Closest existing research.** Li et al. 2026: *"FID is fundamentally trajectory-blind"* —
asserts blindness, measures per-sample kinetic energy, but never measures map error against
a ground-truth map, and gives no decomposition explaining *why* blindness is structural
rather than incidental. Vaitl & Klein 2025 observe FM MSE *"largely unchanged"* while
sampling efficiency improves — an unexplained hint of the same decoupling.

**Minimum distinguishing experiment.** E1 (shares all machinery) + a capacity/checkpoint
sweep. **Cost:** ~1 CPU-hour on top of E1.

**Confounders.** (a) **Solver error** — must be excluded (H4). (b) `W₂` estimator bias at
finite sample size — handled by subtracting an explicitly measured same-`n` floor.
(c) `Φ*` is only "ground truth" for the *population* field; if the model generalises
differently from `v*`, `M` conflates error with legitimate generalisation. **Handled by
training on a fresh-sample (effectively infinite-data) stream so that `v*` really is the
regression target** — this is the single most important design decision.

### H3 — Divergence alignment improves the visible half only (differentiates us from the closest method)

**Claim.** Adding the conditional divergence-matching loss of Huang et al. (ICML 2025) at
tuned `λ` reduces the gradient component `‖∇φ‖` and the endpoint `W₂`, but does **not**
reduce `‖r‖` or `M` by a comparable factor; `f` therefore *increases* under FDM.

**Mathematical reasoning.** `L_CDM` penalises `|∇·(u−v) + (u−v)·∇log p|`, which annihilates
flux-null perturbations by construction. It is blind to `r` in exactly the same way `W₂` is.

**Predicted observation.** FDM: `W₂` ↓, `M` ≈ unchanged, `f` ↑.

**Falsifying result.** FDM reduces `M` proportionally to `W₂` — then divergence alignment
already solves the problem and there is nothing to report.

**Honest prior.** ~70%. Nearly a corollary of the algebra, but worth running because it is
the one experiment that cleanly separates our claim from the closest *method* paper.

**Closest existing research.** Huang et al. 2025 — they report only distributional metrics,
verified by full-text read. **Minimum experiment:** retrain with `λ₂ > 0`, measure the same
quantities. **Cost:** ~1 CPU-hour. **Confounders:** fair compute budget (same steps, same
architecture, tuned `λ` by grid); FDM's extra per-step cost must be reported, not hidden.

---

### H4 — Null hypothesis / mandatory control: the differences are solver artifacts

**Claim to be refuted.** All observed trajectory and map differences are produced by ODE
discretisation, not by differences in the learned fields.

**Predicted observation if H4 is true.** `M` and between-seed map disagreement shrink
towards zero as `rtol` is tightened from `1e-5` to `1e-10`.

**Refutation criterion (predeclared).** `M` changes by `< 10%` relative between
`rtol = 1e-5` and `rtol = 1e-10`, and the between-seed disagreement changes by `< 10%`.

**Status.** Not a research contribution — a gate. If H4 is not refuted, H1/H2 are dead and
we stop (Phase 7 criterion "effect disappears with correct numerical integration").

## 3. DECISION

**Primary hypothesis: H1.** H2 is adopted as the *consequence* claim and is measured from
the same runs at near-zero marginal cost. H4 is a mandatory gate run first. H3 is deferred
until H1/H2 have an outcome.

**Rationale.** H1 scores highest on evidential clarity and novelty, is cheap, and — unlike
H2 — has a genuinely uncertain outcome (my stated prior is only ~40%). It is the only
candidate whose result is informative in *both* directions: `f > f_0` localises a real
failure mode; `f ≈ f_0` falsifies the "networks exploit the freedom" reading of the
starting idea while still establishing H2's blindness factor as a structural, not
incidental, fact. H3 is a cheap follow-up that only matters if H1/H2 survive.

### Pre-registered decisive experiment E1 (declared BEFORE any training run)

**Testbed.** `p_1` = 2-D Gaussian mixture, 8 equal components on a radius-4 ring,
`σ = 0.3`; `p_0 = N(0,I)`; independent coupling; linear interpolant. `v*` and `p_t` in
closed form (`theory.md` §4). **Training uses a fresh i.i.d. sample every step** so the
regression target is the population `v*` and memorisation/generalisation is not a
confounder.

**Models.** 8 seeds × MLP(width 128, 3 hidden, SiLU, sinusoidal `t`-embedding), trained to
convergence; capacity sweep {64, 128, 256} for H2.

**PRIMARY METRIC.** `f` = time-integrated flux-null fraction of held-out velocity error,
computed with the exactly-orthogonal finite-volume weighted-Helmholtz discretisation
(`theory.md` §5), on `t ∈ [0, 0.98]`, grid `256²`, domain `[−8,8]²`.

**MATCHED NULL `f_0`.** 8 randomly-initialised networks of the *same* architecture,
rescaled to the same `L²(dt⊗p_t)` norm as the measured residuals, decomposed by the
identical operator. This controls for smoothness/spectrum, which a naive `f_0 = ½`
(dimension-counting) argument does not.

**PRE-REGISTERED SUCCESS CRITERION.**
* **H1 supported** iff `f̄ − f̄_0 > 0.05` AND Welch two-sample `p < 0.01` over 8+8 seeds
  AND `|f̄(grid 192) − f̄(grid 256)| < 0.01`.
* **H1 falsified** iff `|f̄ − f̄_0| ≤ 0.05`, or `f̄ < f̄_0`.
* **H1 inconclusive** iff significant but effect `< 0.05`, or grid-instability `> 0.01`.

**SECONDARY, pre-registered.** `R = M / (W₂ − W₂_floor) ≥ 3` for the median seed ⟹ H2
supported. `W₂_floor` = `W₂` between two independent `n`-samples of `p_1` at the same `n`.

**GATE (run first).** H4: `M` and between-seed map disagreement must each change `< 10%`
between `rtol = 1e-5` and `1e-10`.

**Interpretation declared in advance** (so thresholds cannot be moved later):
* `f̄ ≈ f̄_0`: the residual is unstructured. The strong reading of the starting hypothesis
  ("networks exploit the non-uniqueness") is **false**. Report as a negative result; H2
  becomes the paper's claim, if anything.
* `f̄ ≫ f̄_0`: networks preferentially err in marginal-preserving directions. Consequential
  and novel; proceed to H3 and to spatial/temporal localisation.
* `f̄ ≪ f̄_0`: networks preferentially err in marginal-*changing* directions ⟹ distributional
  metrics are *better* proxies than theory suggests. Also publishable, and would refute H2.

**Budget allocated to E1:** ≤ 3 CPU-hours. Abandon if exceeded without a clean gate pass.

## 5. VERDICTS (2026-10-24, after E0/E1/E2b)

### H4 (gate) — **REFUTED as an explanation, i.e. GATE PASSED**
`M` changes by 0.01 % between `rtol = 10⁻⁵` and `10⁻⁹` (0.01966 → 0.01966), as does the
between-seed map disagreement. Threshold was < 10 %. The observed trajectory differences
are properties of the learned fields, not of the integrator. Phase-7 stop criterion
"effect disappears with correct numerical integration" does **not** trigger.

### H1 — **FALSIFIED, in the direction opposite to the hypothesis**
H1 predicted `f > f₀ + 0.05`: that networks preferentially err in marginal-preserving
directions. Measured `f = 0.1402 ± 0.0319` against a properly matched null
`f₀ = 0.7201 ± 0.0367` (`p = 1.3×10⁻¹⁴`). So networks err **overwhelmingly in
marginal-CHANGING directions** — they largely inherit the exact curl-free structure of the
target. Confirmed independently and grid-free by the Jacobian asymmetry
`κ = 0.0304 ± 0.0041` for trained nets vs `0.803 ± 0.336` at random init (`κ(v*) = 0`).

**Pre-registration failure, declared.** Against the *pre-registered* null (`null_init`,
randomly-initialised nets) the criterion is formally met (`f − f₀ = +0.135`,
`p = 2.7×10⁻⁶`). We do **not** claim H1 as supported on that basis, because post-hoc
diagnosis shows `null_init` is degenerate: the constant (bias) component of a
randomly-initialised net carries 89–99.5 % of its field energy, and a constant field is a
trivial gradient. The null therefore measured the wrong thing. This is recorded as a
**failure of our pre-registered null design**, not as a positive result. The two matched
nulls (which preserve the measured residual's magnitude field, spectrum and energy exactly
and randomise only direction) are the ones we interpret. Lesson: a null model must be
validated for non-degeneracy *before* the decisive run, exactly as the measurement
instruments were in E0.

**This falsifies the brief's starting hypothesis in its strong form.** Learned
flow-matching models do not exploit the velocity-field freedom; they suppress it by a
factor of ~5 in energy (~26× in local rotationality) relative to chance.

### H2 — **SUPPORTED, with a narrower claim than pre-registered**
The pre-registered `W₂`-based test (`R ≥ 3`) was **not evaluable**: `W₂` at `n = 4000` in
2-D cannot resolve even the ground-truth map (`W₂(Φ*#p₀,p₁) = 0.387` exceeds the
two-sample floor 0.367). Re-run with a validated KL instrument (resolves 10⁻⁷ nats;
reads exactly 0 on the true field), E2b shows: injecting the *measured* flux-null
component of a *trained* net's error into the true field gives `|KL| ≤ 0.007`
(indistinguishable from the true field, 4× below the full model's own 0.018) while
`M = 0.0106 ± 0.0004`, worst-particle displacement up to 42 % of the data radius, and
**0.31 % of samples re-routed to a different mode** at essentially unchanged mode occupancy.

**Scope restriction imposed by the evidence.** The components do **not** act additively on
the marginals (`KL(Φ_∇φ) = 0.148`, 8× the full model's — because `r` is flux-null w.r.t.
`p_t`, not w.r.t. the path `v* + ∇φ` generates). We therefore may **not** claim "a
fraction `f` of the model's map error is invisible". The supported claim is only the
injection claim above.

### H3 — **not run.** Deferred as planned, and now largely moot: H1's falsification means
divergence alignment is not the interesting lever. Recorded as open.

### New hypothesis adopted post hoc and tested (E3): curl-free parameterisation
Because `v*` is exactly a gradient, `v_θ = ∇Ψ_θ` is a zero-bias restriction. Pre-registered
predictions Q1–Q4 are in `src/e3_curlfree.py`. **Flagged as post-hoc**: it was motivated by
a mid-project measurement, not declared in §3, and the architecture itself is prior work
(Action Matching; Energy Matching). Only the measurement is ours.
